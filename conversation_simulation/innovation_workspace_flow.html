Innovation Workspace — How It Works

A fictional example: developing a self-cooling building facade inspired by termite mound ventilation

1
AI generates a starting concept

Cross-domain analogical reasoning discovers a mechanism in nature and transfers it to the problem.

TermoFacade: Phase-Change Ventilation Panel

A building facade panel that uses convective chimney channels — inspired by termite mound thermoregulation — to passively cool interior spaces. Channels filled with microencapsulated phase-change material (PCM) absorb heat during the day and release it at night via stack-effect ventilation.

Mechanism source: Termite Mound Passive Thermoregulation → Stack-Effect Convective Cooling
2
AI stress-tests the concept

The AI systematically probes the concept — checking physics, feasibility, cost, and competitive position. Each failure extracts a constraint that narrows the search for what comes next.

PCM leakage under thermal cycling — KILLED

Microencapsulated paraffin PCM shells crack after ~200 thermal cycles (Li et al. 2021). Building facades require 10,000+ cycles over 20-year lifespan.

Kill evidence: 50x shortfall in cycle durability
Bulk PCM in structural channels is not viable — encapsulation fails at building-scale cycle counts

PIVOT → Shape-stabilized PCM in porous matrix

Embed paraffin in expanded graphite or diatomite matrix — no shell to crack. Zhang 2019: 500+ cycles with <3% capacity loss.

Insufficient airflow in narrow channels — KILLED

Stack effect through 2cm channels at ΔT=8°C: only 0.3 m/s airflow. Heat removal rate: ~15 W/m². Cooling load for office space: 50-80 W/m². Off by 4x.

Kill evidence: 4x shortfall in cooling capacity vs. requirement
Passive stack-effect alone cannot meet commercial building cooling loads — needs active assist or radically different geometry

REFRAME → PCM as thermal buffer, not primary cooling

Stop trying to replace HVAC. Instead: PCM absorbs peak load (2-4 PM), shifts it to off-peak night ventilation. Reduces HVAC sizing by 30-40%, not eliminates it.

DISCOVERY: Radiative cooling surface + PCM = hybrid

AI finds a second mechanism: daytime radiative sky cooling (Raman et al. 2014, Stanford). Emits thermal IR through atmosphere's 8-13μm transparency window. Combined with PCM thermal buffer → passive cooling that actually reaches 50+ W/m².

Radiative film durability outdoors — KILLED as standalone

Polymer multilayer radiative films degrade under UV within 2-3 years. Building facades need 20+ year life. But: protective glazing layer solves this at +$8/m² cost.

Resolved with glazing — added to architecture as protective layer

SURVIVES: Hybrid radiative-PCM facade panel

Glazed radiative cooling surface (daytime) + shape-stabilized PCM thermal buffer (peak shifting) + natural ventilation channels (night flush). Projected: 55 W/m² passive cooling, 35% HVAC reduction, $120/m² installed cost vs. $90/m² for standard curtain wall.

AI has mapped the design space — 3 dead ends, 2 pivots, 1 survivor with a specific architecture. Now the expert enters.
3
Expert adds constraints, taste, and real-world knowledge

The expert sees the full exploration tree — what was tried, what was killed and why, what survived. They don't start from scratch. They build on the map and add what only they know.

Chat — Dr. Reyes, Building Envelope Engineer
Expert

The PCM reframe is smart, but you're assuming we can get expanded graphite panels through fire code. What's the flame spread rating? ASTM E84 Class A is non-negotiable for exterior facades.

AI

Good catch. Paraffin/expanded-graphite composites: Class B at best (flame spread index ~40-60). Class A requires FSI ≤ 25. Two options: (1) switch to salt hydrate PCM (non-flammable, CaCl₂·6H₂O), trades 15% latent heat capacity but passes fire code; (2) add intumescent coating at +$6/m².

Expert

Salt hydrate. We've used CaCl₂ PCM on the Denver project — it works but has a phase segregation problem after ~1000 cycles. Check if anyone's solved that since 2022.

AI

Found it: Xie et al. 2024 — thickened CaCl₂·6H₂O with 2% hydroxyethyl cellulose + 0.5% SrCl₂ nucleator. 5,000 cycles, <5% capacity fade. The nucleator prevents supercooling too. Updated the architecture.

Expert

Now the real question — what does this cost vs. a Kingspan insulated panel with standard HVAC? Because my client won't pay a 30% premium for a facade, no matter how clever it is.

AI

Facade alone: $120/m² vs $90/m² Kingspan (+33%). But lifecycle: the 35% HVAC reduction saves ~$12/m²/year in a Phoenix climate zone. Payback: 2.5 years. Over 20-year building life: net savings of $150/m². I can model this for your specific climate zone if you share the location.

Canvas: the structured residue

The canvas captures everything that's been explored — kills, constraints, pivots, expert additions — as a navigable record for the team.

Dead ends (3 killed)

  • Microencapsulated PCM — shell cracking at cycle count
  • Passive stack-effect only — 4x short on cooling capacity
  • Polymer radiative film — UV degradation (resolved with glazing)

Constraints discovered

  • PCM must be shape-stabilized, not encapsulated
  • Passive cooling alone won't replace HVAC — reframe as peak-load buffer
  • ASTM E84 Class A required — no paraffin (expert added)
  • Must beat Kingspan on 20-year lifecycle cost (expert added)

Surviving architecture

  • Glazed radiative cooling surface (daytime passive)
  • CaCl₂·6H₂O / HEC / SrCl₂ PCM layer (peak shifting)
  • Natural ventilation channels (night flush)
  • Payback: 2.5 years in hot climates

Next: gating experiments
